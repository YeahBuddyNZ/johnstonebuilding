New build · Ōmaha

Headland House

A cedar-clad home stepped down a coastal site, built to take forty years of salt air without looking like it is trying.

Type
New build
Location
Ōmaha
Completed
2024
Design
Architect-designed
Scale
Four bedrooms, 320m²
Role
Main contractor

A house on an exposed headland has one job before any other: stay dry, stay tight, and keep looking considered while it does it.

The site fell away steeply to the north, so the build was set out as three stepped platforms rather than a single cut-and-fill terrace. That decision cost more in foundations and saved the whole character of the site — the house sits in the slope instead of on top of a retaining wall.

Vertical cedar was pre-oiled on all four faces before it went up, over a fully drained and ventilated cavity, with every fixing set out to a grid so the elevation reads as one surface rather than a series of boards. Junctions at the corners were mitred rather than trimmed.

The detail

Where the time went.

The long north elevation has no visible flashing across a fourteen-metre run. Getting there meant setting out the cladding, the joinery head heights and the soffit line together, before the frame was closed in, and holding all three trades to it.
